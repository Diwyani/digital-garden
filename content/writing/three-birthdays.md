*On growing older*

## 2023 — Birthday (Courage)

I was in Bir, Himachal Pradesh. First solo trip. The courage was a gift to myself. I was fed up of leading a fear-based life. The map I had for my life had been torn apart a few months ago, and I hadn't found a new one yet.

When the clock hit midnight, I cried. A lot. And then lightning started striking in every direction, filling the whole sky, filling my little library-esque cottage with light. And I remember thinking — wow, how lucky am I to witness this. What they say is true: how we deal with micro joy is how we deal with macro grief. The next morning I watched the sky put on a show. I was even happier at sunset.

*The thought behind going solo: I'm done with living fear-based. Let's practice existential crisis and see where it takes me.*

## 2024 — Birthday (Lost)

No pictures from this birthday. That's rare for me.

I was anxious in a way that had no clean edges — not about one specific thing, just this low hum of *what am I doing, where am I going, is this it?* Same job. Same house. No companionship. I kept asking people older than me: what is the right way to live? How do you navigate?

Nobody gave me a satisfying answer. Because there isn't one. But I wasn't ready to hear that yet. I was desperate for an anchor. Desperate for stable ground. I wanted someone or something to just tell me — and I would have listened to almost anyone.

What I got instead was a throwaway line from a coworker. He said:

> "If you're not happy, you have to change things either around you or within you."

## 2025 — Birthday (Grounded)

Same job. Same house. No companionship. But the change my coworker talked about has arrived within. I have cultivated a deep sense of happiness, an unshakable sense of security. Nothing shakes the ground I stand on — but the ground keeps changing, and I'm somehow okay with all of it.

*From my journal on the birthday, 14th Oct 2025:*

> Walked 2k steps after 8 weeks. Watched a peacock flight, shimmering water, 2 uncles taking photos of each other rather than of flowers (must be in their 60s), reflection of water, a rainbow by the fountain, eagle flight, very sexy trees, duck in big glides. Thumpu Soda, Neer dosa (should have gone for rava dosa lol).

## The Map

Sense of security sits at the top. Everything flows from it, and everything feeds back into it. There are two sides to it — one you build alone, and one you build with people.

**The inner side: self-love**
Self-love isn't a feeling you wait for. It's a set of actions. It's doing what you said you would do. Taking care of yourself — the mundane things, the walks, the meals, the basics you keep skipping. Being honest with yourself, at least. Saying no. Removing yourself from bad spaces, bad people, bad versions of yourself you've outgrown. Detachment when needed. Being kind to people but stopping the pleasing, stopping the folding of yourself to fit in.

All of that builds self-esteem, which builds confidence, which builds self-trust. It's a loop. You act, you trust yourself more, you act again.

At the center of self-love is **acceptance** — of where you are, who you are, and what is actually happening. Not the story you're telling yourself about it. What's actually there.

**The outer side: outsourced love**
This one is for the bad days. This is your friends, your family, your people. But not everyone qualifies. The ones who uplift you, who stand in your storms — those are your people. The ones who drain you, mooch off you, never show up — that's an exit, not a relationship.

And the way you find your people? You build the village first. You show up. You be the friend. You make it easy for people. Reliability is magnetic.

It's a lot of work, and it will take a lot of time. There will be days when you're crumbling — it's an identity shift. You will be in a space of becoming for a long while. You will mourn the old self. But in the end, it will all be worth it.

I still don't have a map. I still don't know where I'm going. But nothing can shake the ground I stand on — it keeps changing, and I'm somehow okay with all of it.
